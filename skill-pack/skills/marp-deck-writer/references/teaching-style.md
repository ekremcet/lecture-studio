# Teaching style (an example, condensed from one presenter's TEACHING_STYLE.md)

The presenter's own style is the `style` paragraph of `studio.json` at the repo root, and `TEACHING_STYLE.md` there when it exists; read those first and follow them. This file shows what such a style looks like when it is spelled out, and the slide-level habits below are good defaults for any lecture deck. Do not apply the policies of the first section to a presenter whose profile says otherwise.

## Principles that must show in the slides

- Learning over memorization. Exams are open-book, open-laptop, open-AI. "Copying an AI answer without understanding earns minimal credit."
- Autonomy and respect. No attendance tracking. "You are adults making your own educational choices." Presence by choice.
- Practical focus. Industry cases: NASA Mars Climate Orbiter ($125M unit bug), Therac-25, Slack notification architecture, Google search scale, Netflix, Instagram. Trade-offs over right answers. "No method fits all."
- Economic reality. "It is 99% about the money." Developer time costs more than compute. CAPEX vs OPEX.
- Engagement over endurance. Pomodoro lectures: 20 minutes on, 5 minutes off; a long break after every two blocks. "We all have the attention span of a goldfish." Breaks are guilt-free.

## Slide-level habits

- Two-column comparisons. Left: concept. Right: example. Or left: Do ✅, right: Don't ❌.
- Code with both a correct and an anti-pattern version. Progressive complexity: simple first.
- Memory layouts as tables (address, content, value). Step-by-step algorithm traces, one step per slide or a numbered list.
- Rhetorical questions and "What happens when..." scenarios; quotes from Wirth, Knuth, Brooks, Strang.
- Question slide then Answer slide. Pop quizzes mid-block.
- Practice or Studio slides with a timebox, a required output, and a debrief. Reveal the sample answer only on the next slide.
- Memes to break tension, not to replace content.

## Deck skeleton

1. Title (course code, course name, week and topic, instructor, date).
2. Recap of last week plus today's focus (weeks 2+). Week 1: course overview, policies, grading.
3. Agenda or learning outcomes.
4. Six blocks of content. Each block: concept slides, one worked example, one retrieval check or practice.
5. Summary or key takeaways.
6. Next week preview with the reading.
7. Thank-you slide with the contact block and the next class date and topic.

## Voice

Direct, second person, short sentences. British or American spelling is fine but consistent within a deck. Bold the key term once per slide. Avoid "crucial", "seamlessly", "game-changer", "leverage" as a verb.
